# Reporting Experimental Statistics

Read this when the piece compares conditions, reports measurement error, or draws a conclusion from numbers. Compute with a script from the raw data and keep the script; do not do statistics in prose.

## 1. Before Any Test

- State the unit of analysis (image, subject, trial, run) and the count for each condition.
- State how the data were split or sampled, how many repetitions there were, and what was fixed.
- Decide the comparison before looking at the outcome when possible. If a comparison was chosen after seeing the data, say so.
- Look at the raw values or a plot first. A mean hides outliers, ceilings and bimodal results.

## 2. What To Report For Every Number

| Quantity | Report with it |
|---|---|
| Mean | standard deviation and n; a confidence interval when an inference is made |
| Median | interquartile range or min-max, and n |
| Proportion or rate | the counts behind it (k of n) and the size of one unit (one item out of 50 is 2 percentage points) |
| Difference between conditions | the two values, the signed difference, its unit, and an interval or a statement of what variation it should be compared with |
| Measured vs theoretical value | both values and the relative error, with the formula used |
| Fit | the model, the fitted parameters with units, and a goodness-of-fit measure |

Use percentage points for the difference between two percentages. Keep the sign of a difference. Use the number of decimals the guideline asks for; otherwise no more digits than the measurement supports.

## 3. Uncertainty With Small Samples

- For a proportion p measured on n items, the binomial standard error is sqrt(p(1-p)/n). With n = 100 and p = 0.5 it is 5 percentage points, so a difference of a few points between two such numbers is within noise.
- Two methods measured on the same items are paired. Compare them item by item (which items one got right and the other wrong), not as two independent proportions.
- A single run gives no estimate of run-to-run variation. Say so instead of implying stability.
- When repetitions exist, report all of them or their mean and spread, not the best one.

## 4. Choosing A Test

| Situation | Usual choice |
|---|---|
| Two independent groups, roughly normal | Welch's t-test |
| Two paired measurements | paired t-test; Wilcoxon signed-rank when not normal |
| Two independent groups, not normal or ordinal | Mann-Whitney U |
| Three or more groups | one-way ANOVA with a post-hoc test; Kruskal-Wallis when not normal |
| Two categorical variables | chi-square; Fisher's exact test with small counts |
| Two yes/no outcomes on the same items | McNemar's test |
| Relationship between two continuous variables | Pearson r, or Spearman rho when not linear or not normal |

Report the test, the statistic, the degrees of freedom where they apply, the exact p-value, and an effect size (Cohen's d, a difference with its interval, an odds ratio, r). A p-value alone is not a result.

Do not run a test the design cannot support. With one run per condition there is nothing to test; describe the difference and its resolution instead.

## 5. Multiple Comparisons And Selection

- Count the comparisons made. With several, correct for it (Holm or Bonferroni) or state that the comparisons are descriptive.
- Data used to choose a model, a setting or a threshold cannot also give an unbiased estimate of its performance. Keep a held-out set that plays no part in any choice and evaluate on it once.
- Do not tune on the test set, and do not use it to break ties.

## 6. Wording

- "Higher in this experiment" is a description. "Better" in general needs more than one setting, sample or dataset.
- Not significant does not mean no effect. Report the interval so the reader sees what sizes of effect remain possible.
- Name confounds: when two things changed together (dose and duration, sample size and measurement time), the result cannot be assigned to one of them.
- State what was not tested, and limit the conclusion to the conditions that were.

## 7. Final Checks For This Genre

1. Every reported statistic can be regenerated by the saved script.
2. n is stated for every number.
3. Every difference has a sign, a unit and something to judge its size against.
4. Claims of significance name the test and give the statistic and exact p-value.
5. The conclusion does not go beyond the tested conditions.
